## How do you log in without getting the account locked?

Use this order. Skipping step one is the most common way a freshly bought account dies.

1. **Sign in through the App Store, or through Settings → your name → Media & Purchases.** Do not use the Apple ID field at the very top of Settings. That field attaches iCloud, device backups and Find My to an account you did not create and cannot fully verify.
2. **If Apple pushes you to upgrade to two-factor authentication, choose "Other options", then "Don't upgrade".** The login goes through, and you keep the account in the state you bought it.
3. **Change the password immediately, then bind your own phone number.** Do the password first only if the listing already gives you a working recovery path; otherwise bind the phone first so a reset code reaches you and not the seller.
4. **Save any verification link that came with the order.** Listings with 2FA usually include a page where you can read the SMS codes. That link is part of the goods.
5. **Download what you came for, then switch back to your own Apple ID** before you open the app or buy anything inside it. Updates and in-app purchases follow the ID that downloaded the app.

## The warranty is six hours, and it excludes the one thing bulk buyers do

Most listings state a warranty window, and one widely sold US listing spells it out as **6 hours**. The clock starts at delivery, not at your first login, which means a Friday purchase checked on Sunday is already out of scope.

What the window covers: a rejected password, a locked or disabled account, or a demand for verification details that were not in the delivery. The remedy is a ticket from the order page with a screenshot attached, submitted before the window closes. Resolution is a replacement from the same listing, or a refund if that listing has sold out.

What it does not cover: bans triggered by bulk or automated activity. If you buy fifty accounts and run them through a script, the warranty is over the moment you start. The storefront's own wording is blunt about permanence too. Overseas accounts are not guaranteed to last forever, Apple can reclaim or lock any account at any time, and the platform disclaims responsibility when that happens.

That combination sets your behaviour. Log in the same day you receive the account, screenshot the first successful App Store sign-in, and keep the ticket open until you have downloaded something.

## Who should not buy a US Apple ID at all

Apple's terms do not permit transferring an Apple ID from one person to another, and Apple can require re-verification or lock an account without warning. Buying one is a workaround with a known failure mode, not a permanent transfer of ownership.

Three groups should register their own account instead of buying:

- **Anyone who wants it as a primary Apple ID**, with iCloud backups, Find My and a personal credit card attached. You cannot attach a card you own to an identity you don't, and Apple can ask for verification at any point.
- **Anyone who needs to pay for subscriptions.** Buying an account does not give you a US payment method. If that is the actual problem, an account purchase solves nothing.
- **Anyone buying in volume for automation.** The warranty excludes exactly that use, so you are self-insuring from the first login.

There is a fourth, softer case: buyers chasing "higher trust score" or "better app compatibility" from aged accounts. Those phrases come from seller copy, and no third party in the listings I checked verifies them.
